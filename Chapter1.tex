\section{ Business Problem Overview and Dataset Provenance}

\subsection{Executive Overview and Business Context}
In the contemporary telecommunications landscape, market saturation and low switching barriers have fundamentally shifted strategic business priorities from aggressive customer acquisition to proactive customer retention. Customer churn---defined as the phenomenon where a subscriber terminates their relationship or service agreement with a provider---represents one of the most critical threats to recurring revenue stability and long-term corporate valuation. Empirical industry studies consistently demonstrate that acquiring a new telecommunications subscriber is five to seven times more capital-intensive than retaining an existing one, due to substantial marketing overheads, promotional hardware subsidies, and physical infrastructure provisioning costs.

In this report, we present an exhaustive technical and strategic examination of the IBM Telco Customer Churn data. Our primary objectives in this study are structured logically across three chapters:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Chapter 1 (Business Problem \& Provenance):} Establish the strategic context of telecommunications churn, introduce the origin and scale of the dataset, and inspect the initial storage schema.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 2 (Feature Taxonomy \& Baseline Statistics):} Classify all $21$ attributes into five functional dimensions, document structural dependencies across service variables, and analyze the baseline descriptive statistics of the quantitative features.
    \item \textbf{Chapter 3 (Data Cleaning \& Sanitization Methodology):} Detail the exact programmatic procedures executed to remove non-predictive identifiers, coerce disguised string variables into numeric types, and handle hidden missing values without inducing data leakage.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Domain Context and Dataset Provenance}
The data we examined in our study is the public benchmark \textit{IBM Telco Customer Churn} dataset, retrieved directly from the official IBM Cloud Pak for Data repository (\texttt{Telco-Customer-Churn.csv}). It models a fictional yet highly realistic telecommunications provider offering residential landline telephone and broadband internet services to households. Each observational record in the data represents a unique individual customer account, capturing a cross-sectional snapshot at a specific point in time of the subscriber's demographic profile, subscribed service portfolio, contractual obligations, billing preferences, financial expenditure, and current retention status.

\subsection{Raw Dataset Scale and Initial Storage Schema}
Prior to any data manipulation or cleaning, our raw dataset comprises exactly $7,043$ rows (individual customer records) and $21$ columns (distinct attributes), occupying approximately $1.1+$ MB of memory in a standard \texttt{pandas} DataFrame structure. 

When ingested into Python via \texttt{pd.read\_csv()}, \texttt{pandas} classifies the $21$ columns into three underlying storage data types (\texttt{dtypes}), as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:raw_schema}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Floating-Point Numeric (\texttt{float64} --- 1 column):} \texttt{MonthlyCharges}, which stores decimal currency figures.
    \item \textbf{Integer Numeric (\texttt{int64} --- 2 columns):} \texttt{SeniorCitizen} (encoded as $0$ or $1$) and \texttt{tenure} (encoded as whole months from $0$ to $72$).
    \item \textbf{String / Categorical Object (\texttt{object} --- 18 columns):} Includes the account identifier (\texttt{customerID}), $15$ categorical demographic, service, and billing attributes, the target variable (\texttt{Churn}), and one anomalous financial column (\texttt{TotalCharges}).
\end{itemize}
